### What the skill got wrong or lacked

1. **A held step is budgeted at the whole bulk's dry run, however small its group.** Each one-package group was gated as 166 s + 600 s, and took about 40 s. On a host with an hourly quiet range, that over-budget decides when a step may start. The owner's approval for `docker` arrived at 23:54:45Z, and the gate refused the step for running into 00:00–00:07, when the step would have finished by 23:56. A held step's own expected time, from the dry run's per-package figures or a floor, would have let it run.
2. **A refused step doesn't say when it could start.** The operator worked out "hh:07:00 to hh:09:10" by hand from the span and the quiet ranges. `apply.sh` knows both, and could print the next start that passes the gate.
3. **The recovery point's `next` names the object, but not how to find it among the bucket's others.** The owner's console listed 1,486 objects by name, ascending, paginated, and the first page ended at the bucket's oldest object: 30 days old, the lifecycle rule's edge. That read as "today's object is missing", and the apply waited until the agent listed the prefix with the backup's own identity. A `gcloud storage ls -l gs://<bucket>/<prefix><stamp>*` line in `next` would have answered it.
4. **The agent's own confirmation reads were denied under the host's ACL**, twice: `LASTSAVE` after the `SAVE`, and `DBSIZE` before the reboot. The operator user holds `+save` and `+info`, not those. Neither was the skill's doing, but [datastores.md](../../datastores.md) could say that under an ACL, the recovery point's `rdb_written` confirms a `SAVE`, and `INFO keyspace` gives the key count, so no other command is needed.

### Surprises

- **An in-guest reboot is seconds on exe.dev, not minutes:** 9 s from `systemctl reboot` to the kernel's start, 13 s for Redis. The callers' notice said 4–5 minutes, and three of four asked whether the reboot had happened at all.
- **A followed origin moved during the run.** That's no fault of the gate, which compares at the bulk. But a record that stops at "nothing pending" would be wrong by one package: the final `--refresh-into` is what showed it, as on [co-index](2026-11-03-co-index.md) eight days before.
- **Approval latency, not the steps, set the run's length:** 22:32Z to 00:16Z, of which the apply was 9 minutes and the reboot 13 seconds of Redis.
