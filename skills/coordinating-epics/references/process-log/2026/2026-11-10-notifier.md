## Session 2026-11-10 — CannObserv/notifier#116

A child epic of CannObserv/watcher#368 (headless Watcher). Notifier is a multi-tenant notification service. In the cohort's cutover, the dispatching tenant moved from Watcher to Archiver, and this epic held notifier's side of it:
- provision Archiver's tenant, keys and channel copies;
- revoke Watcher's keys after the cutover;
- delete Watcher's tenants later.

None of the children changed service code. Each was database work through the host's credential scripts, a tailnet ACL change, or docs. The service deploys as the main checkout plus a restart of two units.

### Shape

- **Way in:** adopted 2026-10-19, five days after it was filed from the parent design's Steps table. It had 2 children and no cursors. Adoption ran the gate, found lint red on `main`'s tip, and filed the gate as the first child.
- **Children:** 2 at adoption, 4 at the close:
  - the gate (lint);
  - the tenant provision;
  - the key revoke;
  - the tenant delete, split from the revoke at check-in 2 and parked on a date.
- **Check-ins:** in the original thread, adoption and 2 check-ins (2026-10-19). Then the hand-over (2026-10-24). In the backlog epic's thread came about 10 probes, 4 sibling read-backs, the cutover window and its 3 read-backs, and 3 closing check-ins (2026-11-09 to 11-10).
- **Baseline:** 807 passed and 9 skipped at adoption, and 878 passed and 3 skipped at the last child's pre-ship. Almost all the growth came from the backlog epic's children on the same host.
- **Duration:** 22 days. Three weeks of that was waiting on the parent's cutover.

### What was different

- **This epic was the provider side.** Most of its life went to answering sibling coordinators, not briefing implementers. Archiver's coordinator posted three read-back requests, over its deploy and its shadow window, and the cutover added three read-backs promised in the live channel. Each was answered on this epic, with read-only SQL and access-log counts labelled `[measured]`. None asked for a change.
- **The thread changed early, and the epic rode another epic's thread.** On 2026-10-24 the original thread shut down, and the thread already coordinating the host's whole-backlog epic took over. The hand-over comment carried everything not yet on GitHub: one docs error, now filed, and three process-log candidates. The backlog epic recorded a boundary decision that this epic's lines are not copied there, and kept one "Sibling epics" line instead. Every probe after that wrote to both epics' logs from one scan of each.
- **The cutover was watched live.** On the operator's instruction, the coordinator watched the cutover window (instant T) with:
  - a read-only monitor script and three timed read-backs;
  - a live encrypted channel with the Watcher and Archiver agents (`using-mayfly-chat`);
  - a watch policy file written in advance, mapping each anomaly to its response and its venue.

  None of the anomalies fired. Parity was 20/20, both services logged the same T, and Watcher drained 73 minutes after T with no sends after it.
- **The date gate was waived.** The tenant delete was parked until T plus 30 days. The operator first did half of it early, the ACL change, outside any thread. Later the operator waived the gate for the rest: the delete and its docs mirror shipped 26 days early, in one implementer thread.
- **The last debt was dropped, not filed.** "Archiver's channel copies deliver" can only be discharged by Archiver's first real send, and none had come. The coordinator recommended filing a watch issue. The operator chose to close and accept it unverified.

### Findings

**Adoption and briefing**
- **A1. The design's "ops only" step was missing a reachability prerequisite.** Provisioning Archiver's tenant needed a tailnet ACL rule from Archiver's node to notifier's ports, and the design didn't mention it. The coordinator found it by reading the host's tailnet doc while drafting the sibling update. It reached the brief as an addendum, the sibling issue, and a row under Waits. **Rule candidate:** before briefing an ops step that gives another node a credential, check that the node can reach the service, from the host's network doc and the live packet filter.
- **A2. A brief claim taken from a summary line in AGENTS.md was wrong.** The summary said ":9000 refuses development keys with 403". That holds only for a dev-marked key in the production database. A key minted in the dev database gets a 401 there, since production doesn't know it. The implementer's pre-build review caught it, and the docs fix was filed. The existing `[stated]` rule covers this: a summary line in a policy file is a claim, not the code.

**Hand-over and two epics**
- **H1. One record per child held across two epics** (replicator's H1, repeated). The receiving thread put a boundary decision on the backlog epic ("its lines are not copied here"), and from then on ordered this epic from a single Sibling-epics line. No child was ever ticked twice. This is the second run with the rule; see the candidate above.
- **H2. `set-line` refuses a ref that leads two bullets.** On the backlog epic, this epic's ref led both its boundary-decision bullet and its Sibling-epics bullet. `replace-section` on Sibling epics did the job. This worked as designed, and is noted for anyone who hits the refusal.

**Sibling read-backs**
- **S1. A read-back request is answered on the provider's epic, and the requester's issue gets a pointer.** The three posted requests were answered in 43 minutes to 18 hours. Each answer was counts only, since both repos are public. Each stated its limit inline: for example, "notifier stores no inline template strings, so item 2 can only be checked as a shape".
- **S2. A sibling's "reachable" claim was checked against this host's access log** before being written as discharged debt. The log also showed what the claim left out: Archiver's first authenticated calls were 401s, from a key the dev database didn't hold. That was relayed with the operator's OK and fixed on Archiver's side within the day.

**The cutover window**
- **W1. Write the watch policy before the window.** The policy file set out, for each event: what counts as normal, what is an alarm, which reading to take, and where to post (the channel and the parent's cutover issue together). When the session restarted mid-window, the policy and timers survived, and only the background monitor had to be restarted. Nothing fired, so the payoff was speed: each read-back took one command, plus a post drafted from the file.
- **W2. A peer's log token was also a control input.** Watcher's restart matched the cutover line that Archiver posted on the shared issue. Watcher asked the other coordinators never to quote that line verbatim on the issue, only to paraphrase it. **Rule candidate:** in a shared tracker, paraphrase another service's control tokens, never paste them.
- **W3. The channel's operating details stayed off durable stores.** That meant the URL file, peer constraints, and promises such as "per-node request counts in every read-back". They were kept in the session's policy file and turned into GitHub comments before goodbye. After the close, nothing about the channel was needed.

**The parked child**
- **P1. A date gate is a choice about how long to keep things, not a dependency.** The delete waited 30 days after the cutover. The parked line said when, but not what the 30 days protected (a window to roll back, or audit rows the operator might want). When the operator waived the gate, the parked line couldn't say what the waiver gave up. **Rule candidate:** a date-triggered park states what the wait guards against.
- **P2. Part of a parked child can ship outside any thread.** The operator changed the tailnet policy themselves. The coordinator measured it from the packet filter, noted the ACL half as done early on the parked line, and asked one question: does the docs mirror ship now or with the child? The operator chose to ship it with the child. The line then carried a dated decision rather than a stale scope.

**Close-out**
- **C1. Attribute a red job before it blocks a close.** On the final SHA, one audit job was red. Reading the job's log, not the rollup, showed an upstream advisory that had been red on `main` for four days. The backlog epic already tracked it, and a pending Dependabot PR fixed it (merged the same day). It was recorded on both epics, and didn't block this one.
- **C2. A debt only another tenant can discharge.** It was the closing checks' one open item. The skill's rule is that a dated check goes to an issue. This one was undated and triggered from outside, and its owner, Archiver, sees its own failed sends. The operator declined the watch issue. This is recorded as a declined recommendation and is not a rule.

### Ending

Closed 2026-11-10. The final state was re-verified with read-only checks:
- no Watcher tenant in either database;
- no channel or dispatch without a tenant;
- Archiver's channel copies intact;
- both units ready.

The summary comment on the epic lists two observations nobody filed: a leftover Watcher tag in an edge of the tailnet policy owned by another repo, and Archiver's survival checked as database rows only. The parent CannObserv/watcher#368 is coordinated in its own thread, and the operator relays the close. The thread goes on as coordinator for the backlog epic, CannObserv/notifier#139.
