## Session 2026-10-14 — CannObserv/broker#80

A burn-down of broker's whole open backlog, posted on gregoryfoster/skills#348 as "a seventh burn-down". broker is an ops-heavy repo: a Redis node with no HTTP service, whose "deploy" is the working tree its timers run from. The way in was the backlog plus the operator's rule: *"technical correctness and operational stability, then safe feature work; front-load issues where an external repo is waiting on our reply."* As of 2026-10-14 the epic is still open. Its last check-in was 2026-10-14 (CannObserv/broker#91 closed), so this entry is dated by that check-in. Findings are numbered 83 to 159, continuing #348's numbering. One private sibling repo is involved, and it is named here by its role only.

### Shape

- **Way in:** backlog plus a rule. No design doc. A disposition pass over all 12 open issues, with a sibling-tracker read for each.
- **Children:** 12 at creation, plus CannObserv/broker#79, which the coordinator filed during creation for a red CI gate and put first. The epic became CannObserv/broker#80.
- **Slotted mid-run:** about eight follow-ups filed by the implementing threads, among them CannObserv/broker#81, CannObserv/broker#82 (date-gated), CannObserv/broker#86 to CannObserv/broker#89, and CannObserv/broker#91 (adopting upstream skill fixes before the next patch window). A sibling epic in the same repo, CannObserv/broker#92, was referenced but not absorbed.
- **Check-ins:** creation, then 15 check-ins, plus three between-check-in entries for sibling answers relayed by the operator. About 20 children closed. Five threads carried two issues each.
- **Days:** 2026-10-05 to 2026-10-14. Check-ins 8 and 9 were five days apart.
- **Stats:** the node's sourced test baseline moved at nearly every tick, from 303 passed at creation to 782 at the last check-in, with the same four skips.

### What was different

- **Most decay came from sibling trackers.** Seven of 12 issues needed a changed-since-filed comment, and only one of those came from this repo's commits. A grep for the repo's name found none of the others.
- **External waits were settled by posting at creation.** Replies and cross-repo asks went out on day one, so their latency overlapped the queue. Only one item needed broker's work for an outside party.
- **Children kept moving after they closed.** Code-review rounds, edited closing comments, go-live steps and new follow-ups all landed under closed issues, often hours later.
- **The platform edited the epic.** GitHub ticked a closed child's box on some closes and not others.
- **The coordinator acted once itself,** on one field of a production monitor, with approval. It was the only exception to the no-implementing rule.

### Findings that changed the protocol

**Creation**
- Run the host's verify gate once at creation. CI had been red for 27 pushes over 10 days on a skip that couldn't tell a runner from the node, and fixing it became the first item (83).
- Record a pass/skip baseline for live tests, with the environment it needs: 284/11 bare against 303/4 with the env file sourced (84).
- On an ops repo, read every open issue in each sibling repo that touches the host, by subject. A name grep misses them (85).
- Enumerate tokens and repos from the env file, not from memory. A token map with three repos missing made a private consumer's tracker look unreachable (86).
- Re-check every "on hold until X", including vendored skills' docs. Here X was met when a skill shipped, and nothing told the held issue (87).
- A staged artifact's expiry is a deadline. A binary in `/tmp` had about four days left before tmpfiles or a boot cleared it (88).
- Collect everything waiting on the next disruptive step and bundle along it: two issues and a closed child's verification debt shared one reboot (89).
- Split "reply owed" from "work owed", and post the replies at creation (90).
- Check a reply about your own system's alarms against the code before posting. The draft named a threshold the probe doesn't have (91).
- The epic takes a number too, so the watermark must include it (93).

**Verify**
- Verify the whole range since the last verified commit on `origin/main`, with or without the child's number. Read every comment from the close onward, and read an edited closing comment as it is now (95, 102, 128).
- The baseline is the check-in's own re-run, restated in the recipe at every tick. Compare the skip set by identity, and tag every count with its environment (94, 103, 112, 158).
- Verify an off-repo artifact at the layer its consumer reads. A caret range in a manifest was pinned in practice by the lockfile and a test (104).
- Re-measure a time-dependent follow-up at the tick. "Three groups unconfirmed" became one after 40 minutes (116).
- Verify with the signal the repo already chose, and read artifact names as the repo defines them. A `lag` counter and a snapshot-timed object name each looked like a fault (117, 124).
- Closed and live are separate events. The epic line records *landed* and *live*, each with its date (131).
- Check a cache-safety claim against every cache the repo holds, not only the one the child saved (151).

**Harvest**
- A `For #N:` note can refute the next issue's premise. Move it onto that issue as a changed-since-filed comment, with the options reframed (96).
- When an implementer overturns a premise in the brief, record it as the coordinator's error on the child's line and in the log (127).
- Re-derive counts and blast radius from the source when slotting. "6 advisories" was three, each listed under two IDs (142).
- A child can create verification debt. Give it to the next child that can pay it, and name the checks (140).
- When a close says "changes when X lands", check X at the check-in (146).

**Re-scan**
- `updatedAt` doesn't prove a body edit; cross-reference events bump it. Diff the body (100).
- A blocking question mid-check-in makes the earlier scan stale. Ask before scanning, or re-scan the waits right before the write. "Last check-in" is the scan's time (107).
- Look for lines in sibling epics that name this repo, not only issues that mention it (118).
- Re-check unfiled horizon items each tick, like waits (155).
- Check each new issue against the shared-surfaces table when slotting it (143).

**Blockers and gates**
- Strike a wait as soon as its reply is posted, and check the waits list itself at each tick (105).
- When the last answer closes a wait, the waiting child becomes something siblings wait on. Flip the list's direction (110).
- A release-gated slot carries the version last seen, and each tick re-reads it (114).
- When a date gate opens, run its conditions read-only and hand off "held at T" with the readings (133).
- A recurring child's next run date is the deadline for adopting the fixes it filed upstream. Track the upstream issue that changes its procedure, even when nothing is owed back (144, 153).
- Re-read a gated item's inputs just before it becomes next, and post what has settled (152).
- When a cross-repo ask stalls with each side waiting, read the target's tracker for who acts. Whoever holds the credential acts (136).

**Hand-off**
- Cite the nearest proof by the platform facts that decide the risk (layout, build date, restart method), not the first proof on file (98).
- A step owned by the operator may already be done. Probe the live system and brief it as "confirm" (97).
- Read sibling answers against each other. When they conflict, read the artifact both describe before asking again. Check any claim that would imply a grant against the source (108, 109, 113).
- When a cut removes a read a sibling's runbook still uses, write the merge order on the child's queue line (111).
- "Look for a known upstream issue first" is a cheap brief line that paid off (115).
- Re-measure the child's original evidence at hand-off, and look for renames since it was filed (122).
- A doc at its token budget is a shared surface. Sequence the children that grow it (123).
- Brief from the consumer's code at a pinned commit, not from its original ask (129).
- Monitoring for a consumer that doesn't exist yet is a timing decision. Hand it off with the options (130).
- A brief for an alerting change blocks two harms: fault keys built from unstable subjects, and a forced test alert on a production monitor (134).
- Name an irreversible step as "needs your approval in the thread", so the stop is expected (138).
- When another coordinator has briefed a child, add to that brief and don't repeat it (139).
- Check that a tag the brief recommends resolves (148).
- For a doc correction, search the claim's terms, not its old figure (154).
- Fence off an optional owner choice that would change the child's size, and say how big it is (156).

**State and writes**
- Anchor on the last-written body, never on text recalled from a draft. The coordinator's own ref normalization moved its anchors (99).
- Keep the diff baseline under `$HOME`, off the session's `/tmp`, before handing off a reboot of the coordinator's host (101).
- The body diff guards against the platform as well as people. GitHub's auto-tick on close is unreliable, so set every box explicitly and assert each child's box by name (126, 135, 141, 147).
- Every check that guards a write exits non-zero and is `&&`-chained to it. The chain later made a wrong-token 404 harmless (125, 159).
- Normalize every ref to `owner/repo#N`, including short `repo#N`, over the whole body and not only new text (92, 145).
- Insert a queue line at the end of the named tier, and assert the tier heading above it (149).
- Live reads can put sensitive state in the transcript. Extract only the flags or counts needed, and lint briefs for it (120).

**Operator**
- Ask the bundling question before the epic edit, and a design decision one check-in before the item that needs it (106, 121).
- Re-read the tier order against the rule each tick. Record a swap as a numbered tiebreak with its reason (119).
- After a long gap, rank a new issue by how long its problem has been live, not by when it was filed (132).
- The no-implementing rule bends only for an extra-small non-code change that clears an outside wait, made with approval and posted evidence (137).
- When an external wait is one confirmation, ask for it and record where you asked (150).

**Siblings**
- A sibling epic filed in the same repo is referenced, not tracked twice. Pull in only its unblocked children, and write the reorder trigger in advance (157).

### Reversals

- **Which commit to verify.** Check-in 1 read CI off `origin/main` instead of the commit in the closing comment (95). Check-in 4 narrowed that to the newest commit carrying the child's number (102). Check-in 8 found work for the next child in commits with no number, and widened it to the whole range since the last verified commit (128). The range won.
- **Who may tick a box.** Check-in 8 kept an unexplained tick and called its cause unknown (126). Check-in 9 saw no repeat and relied on nothing (135). A third close ticked within a second, so a box-only change on the just-closed child became "expected" (141). Then two closes two seconds apart ticked only one box, and the rule became: the edit sets every box itself and asserts each by name (147). The last version won.
- **The ref checker.** It first skipped code spans and checked new text for bare `#N` (92). It was widened to catch short `repo#N` after one slipped into the log between check-ins 4 and 5, and then run over the whole body, because older log lines held short refs (145). The whole-body check won.
- **The test baseline.** The closing comment's count gave way to the check-in's own re-run (112), and later every count had to carry its environment (158).
- **The no-implementing rule.** It held for the whole run except one approved, recorded change to a monitor field (137). The exception is narrow and excludes code.

### What v1's scripts would and wouldn't have caught

- **Would:** `scan.sh`'s commit range lists unnumbered commits in the range, which covers 128 and 102. Its number watermark caught a follow-up filed two hours after a close, as the hand-run watermark did.
- **Would:** `epic-edit.sh` refuses any body that differs from its baseline except for ticks named in `--accept-ticks`, sets boxes by ref, and anchors by ref rather than recalled text (99, 126, 141, 147). It also inserts at a named section's end (149).
- **Wouldn't:** short `repo#N` refs. The ref pattern doesn't match them, and the lint runs only on introduced lines, so 145's old short refs would stay. The secret lint knows token formats, not a password digest (120).
- **Wouldn't:** posts to other repos (92, 125). `epic-edit.sh` writes only the epic body, so cross-repo replies and briefs still need their own gated check.
- **Wouldn't:** CI and test baselines, sibling trackers read by subject, or lines in sibling epics (84, 85, 118, 136). `scan.sh` reports the states of refs already in the body, not issues that never mention it.
